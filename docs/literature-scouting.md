# Literature scouting

Working catalogue of reference works the project does **not yet
hold** but would benefit from acquiring, plus open-access sources
that can be cited immediately. This is the scouting / shopping
list; `docs/bibliography.md` is the catalogue of works already
evaluated and in use.

Last search pass: 2026-05-20.

---

## Why this matters — the confidence blocker

Seven glossary entries — `kiln`, `mashing`, `distillers-yeast`,
`fermentation`, `reflux`, `shell-and-tube-condenser`,
`wash-still` — are pinned at `confidence: medium` because each
cites only one source (Russell ed. 2014). The project rubric
reserves `high` for claims with multi-source corroboration. One
excellent source is still one source.

Lifting those entries to `high` requires a **second, independent,
credible academic source** covering the same production chain.
"Independent" is the operative word: a newer edition of Russell's
own book does not count.

The task-2 confidence blocker is now resolved — Miller 2024 is

### Piggott, Sharp & Duncan (eds.) — *The Science and Technology of Whiskies* (1989)

- **Editors:** J. R. Piggott, R. Sharp, R. E. B. Duncan
- **Publisher:** Longman Scientific & Technical
- **ISBN:** 0-582-04128-3
- Already catalogued in `docs/bibliography.md`.

The pre-Russell multi-author technical reference. Still valuable
for legacy-practice depth — direct-fired distillation, traditional
malting, the pre-2000s commercial supply chain — that Russell's
2014 edition treats only briefly. Out of print; acquisition is
second-hand or library. The user confirmed on 2026-05-20 that no
digital copy is available, so grounding entries against Piggott
is blocked until a physical or library copy can be consulted.
No longer load-bearing for the confidence promotions (Miller
fills that role), but a useful third corroborating voice and the
source the la Grange-Nel thesis itself leans on.

## Held sources

Reference works the project now holds.

### Miller — *Whisky Science: A Condensed Distillation*, 2nd ed. (2024)

- **Author:** Gregory H. Miller, Professor Emeritus of Chemical
  Engineering, University of California, Davis.
- **Publisher:** Springer; ISBN 978-3-031-50686-4 (print),
  978-3-031-50687-1 (eBook). 1st ed. 2019, 2nd ed. 2024.
- Held with the project (PDF), acquired 2026-05-20.

**The task-2 unblock.** A single-author Springer technical
reference, genuinely independent of the Russell lineage —
different author, publisher, and institutional base (UC Davis
chemical engineering, not Heriot-Watt ICBD) — so it satisfies the
multi-source corroboration the confidence rubric requires. The
2nd edition adds 350+ citations and is heavily primary-sourced.

Its chapter structure covers every one of the seven
medium-confidence glossary entries: Malting (`kiln`), Mashing
(`mashing`), Fermentation (`distillers-yeast`, `fermentation`),
and two distillation chapters — Distillation Experiments &
Observations, and Distillation Theory with explicit sections on
Rayleigh distillation with reflux and triple pot distillation
(`reflux`, `wash-still`, `shell-and-tube-condenser`). It also
covers maturation, copper chemistry, and oxidation mechanisms —
relevant to `copper-conversation` and `cask-maturation-kinetics`.

The task-2 grounding pass is complete (2026-05-20): Miller was
grounded against the seven medium-confidence entries. Kiln,
mashing, fermentation, reflux and wash-still were promoted to
`high`; distillers-yeast and shell-and-tube remain `medium`
(Miller does not corroborate, respectively, the named yeast-house
list and condenser types). See the Miller entry in
`docs/bibliography.md` and the 2026-05-20 TODO Recently-Completed
entry.

### Russell, Stewart & Kellershohn (eds.) — *Whisky and Other Spirits: Technology, Production and Marketing*, 3rd ed. (2022)

- **Editors:** Inge Russell, Graham G. Stewart, Julie Kellershohn
- **Publisher:** Academic Press / Elsevier; © 2022
- **ISBN:** 978-0-12-822076-4
- Held with the project (PDF).

The current edition of the exact book the project cites at its
2nd edition (2014, "Russell ed. 2014"). Substantially
restructured, with new chapters on Japanese, Indian, Canadian,
craft, and Asia-region whiskies.

**It does NOT unblock the confidence promotions**, and the reason
is concrete, not merely "same book lineage": the 3rd-edition
chapters covering the seven medium-confidence entries are written
by the *same authors* as the 2nd edition. Ch. 14 "Batch
distillation" is by Denis A. Nicol — the same Nicol cited from
the 2nd edition for the triple-distillation page — so it cannot
independently corroborate `wash-still`, `reflux`, or
`shell-and-tube-condenser`. Ch. 12 "Distilling yeast and
fermentation" is by the editors Russell & Stewart, so it does not
independently corroborate `distillers-yeast` or `fermentation`.
Same author + same work = not a second source.

What it IS good for:

- **Canonical Russell for new work.** New entries (Ardbeg
  onward) should cite the 3rd edition, not the 2nd.
- **Currency.** For the 14 entries grounded against Russell ed.
  2014, the 3rd edition gives current chapter / page refs. But
  per the project's own confidence-review conclusion, re-grounding
  to a newer edition of the same source is a currency improvement,
  not a confidence change — a low-priority, opportunistic
  migration, not a blanket one.
- **Production-chain chapter map (3rd ed.):** Ch. 10 Scotch raw
  material selection & processing (Bringhurst, Harrison,
  Brosnan); Ch. 11 Water (same); Ch. 12 Distilling yeast and
  fermentation (Russell, Stewart); Ch. 13 Contamination (Wilson);
  Ch. 14 Batch distillation (Nicol); Ch. 15 Grain whisky
  distillation (Murray); Ch. 16 Maturation (Conner); Ch. 17
  Blending (Macleod); Ch. 18 Sensory analysis; Ch. 19 Whisky
  analysis.

The confidence blocker therefore still stands: a genuinely
independent academic source — Miller 2024 or Piggott 1989 — is
still required.

### la Grange-Nel — *Characterisation and Improvement of Whiskey Yeast* (2003)

- **Author:** Karin la Grange-Nel
- **Type:** MSc thesis (Master of Agricultural Sciences),
  University of Stellenbosch, 2003. Supervised by P. van
  Rensburg, M. G. Lambrechts, I. S. Pretorius, Q. Willemse.
- **Access:** open, via the Stellenbosch repository
  (scholar.sun.ac.za); a copy is held with the project.

A Master's thesis, not a peer-reviewed journal article — so it
does NOT on its own satisfy the second-independent-source
requirement for promoting a medium-confidence entry to `high`.
Its value is twofold:

- **Chapter 2 (Literature Review)** is a broad, well-referenced
  production-chain survey — fermentation, flavour/aroma
  compounds, distillation, maturation, yeast strains. Section 2.4
  (fermentation) is directly on-topic for the `distillers-yeast`
  and `fermentation` glossary entries (e.g. the primary/secondary
  yeast-strain practice in Scottish malt distilleries). Citable
  as a *supporting* source alongside Russell.
- Its **reference lists** are a bibliography-mining route to the
  primary peer-reviewed literature — see the next section.

The experimental chapters (yeast transformed with LKA1/LKA2
amylase genes from *Lipomyces kononenkoae*) are research-frontier
GM work, not standard distilling practice, and are not
project-relevant.

## Primary papers to chase

Primary peer-reviewed papers and reference-book chapters mined
from the la Grange-Nel 2003 Chapter 2 reference list, mapped to
the project entries each would help ground. Several *Journal of
the Institute of Brewing* (J. Inst. Brew.) items may be freely
accessible via jib.cibd.org.uk.

Chapters in Piggott, Sharp & Duncan (eds.) 1989, *The Science and
Technology of Whiskies* — acquiring that book (see Recommended
acquisitions above) delivers all of these at once:

- Bathgate & Cook, "Malting of barley for Scotch whiskies",
  pp 19-63 — grounds `kiln` and the malting glossaries.
- Korhola, Harju & Lehtonen, "Fermentation", pp 89-117 —
  grounds `fermentation`, `distillers-yeast`.
- Nicol, "Batch distillation", pp 118-149 — grounds `wash-still`,
  `reflux`, `shell-and-tube-condenser`.
- Nishimura & Matsuyama, "Maturation and maturation chemistry",
  pp 235-263 — grounds `educational/cask-maturation-kinetics`.

Stand-alone peer-reviewed papers:

- Berry, "The physiology and microbiology of Scotch whisky
  fermentations", Prog. Ind. Microbiol. 19:199-243 (1984) —
  `fermentation`, `distillers-yeast`.
- Berry, Lavery & Sim, "Amylase activities during malt whisky
  production", Crit. Rev. Biotechnol. 5:143-157 (1987) —
  `mashing`.
- Bringhurst et al., "The identification and behaviour of
  branched dextrins in the production of Scotch whisky", J. Inst.
  Brew. 107:137-149 (2001) — `mashing` (SWRI authors).
- Barbour & Priest, "Some effects of Lactobacillus contamination
  in Scotch whisky fermentations", J. Inst. Brew. 94:89-92
  (1988) — `fermentation`.
- Makanjuola & Springham, "Identification of lactic acid bacteria
  isolated from different stages of malt whisky distillery
  fermentations", J. Inst. Brew. 90:13-19 (1984) — `fermentation`.
- Geddes & Riffkin, "Influence of lactic acid bacteria on
  aldehyde, ester and higher alcohol formation during Scotch
  whisky fermentations", in *Distilled Beverage Flavour* (1989) —
  `fermentation` and the flavour-compound pages.
- Palmer, "Scientific Review of Scotch Malt Whisky", Ferment
  10:367-379 (1997) — broad review.

## Secondary / lower-priority books

- ***The World Atlas of Whisky*, 3rd ed.** — Dave Broom. Regional
  reference; useful for distillery context, not technical
  corroboration.
- ***The Whisky Dictionary*** — Ian Wisniewski. Glossary-format
  (~144 pp.); could corroborate glossary entries but is more
  popular than academic in register.
- ***The Malt Whisky Yearbook* (recent edition)** — trade
  reference for equipment specs and capacity figures. Not
  academic; firms up distillery citations but does not help the
  confidence blocker.

ed. 2014 precedent (see the 2026-05-19 entry in `TODO.md`
Recently Completed): extract the table of contents, map chapters
to the affected entries, then ground each claim against specific

(Springer, Elsevier/Woodhead) for new whisky-science titles, and
