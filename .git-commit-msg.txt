distilleries: Macallan — 15th distillery, sherry-cask chain

The third populated Speyside entry (after Glenfarclas and
Glenfiddich) and the project's first sherry-cask-led house style
recorded against a major-scale operation.

Why Macallan
------------

The previous wave (Ardbeg) completed the four-distillery
south-Islay heavily-peated cluster (Ardbeg / Laphroaig /
Lagavulin / Caol Ila). The obvious next gap was a Speyside
heavyweight; between Glenlivet (market scale) and Macallan
(sherry-led house style), Macallan was the higher-information
choice because the project's data layer already covers the
sherry-cask chain at the cask-vocabulary level (oloroso-sherry-
butt, pedro-ximenez-sherry-butt) but not yet at a distillery
whose entire operation is built around it. Macallan exercises
that integration end-to-end, including Edrington's vertical
move into Jerez cooperage supply.

Site identity
-------------

- The Macallan, Easter Elchies estate, Craigellachie, Moray;
  Speyside under the SWA 2009 regional designation.
- Founded 1824 by Alexander Reid under the Excise Act 1823.
- Edrington-owned since 1999 (via Highland Distillers
  acquisition); Edrington itself voting-controlled by The
  Robertson Trust, a Scottish charitable trust — an ownership
  structure unusual at this scale in the industry.
- New 2018 still house designed by Rogers Stirk Harbour +
  Partners (£140m capital project, RIBA Stirling Prize 2019
  shortlist, 2019 RIAS Andrew Doolan Best Building in Scotland).
- 36 stills (12 wash @ 13,000 L + 24 spirit @ 3,900 L) at
  ~15M LPA. The spirit stills are among the smallest in
  Scotland — a long-standing piece of the brand's positioning,
  branded by the producer as "curiously small stills".

House style
-----------

Sherry-led. The Sherry Oak core range matured exclusively in
oloroso-seasoned oak sherry casks from Jerez (historically
European-oak dominant). The 2004 Fine Oak range — renamed
Triple Cask Matured in 2018 — added American-oak ex-bourbon
casks alongside the European- and American-oak sherry
components, giving a three-cask vatting unique among the core
Macallan ranges. The Double Cask range sits between these,
vatting two oloroso-seasoned cask types (European- and American-
oak) but no ex-bourbon component.

In September 2023, Edrington acquired Vasyma, a Jerez-based
cooperage — a vertical move into sherry-cask supply directly
relevant to the Macallan style.

Bottlings
---------

Three bottlings recorded, picked to stretch the cask chain:

- **macallan-sherry-oak-12** — the canonical Macallan sherry
  expression. Oloroso-seasoned oak sherry casks, single style.
  43% ABV. The comparison point for the other two.
- **macallan-double-cask-12** — vats European-oak oloroso and
  American-oak oloroso. Both sherry-seasoned, no ex-bourbon.
  Sits between Sherry Oak (single-style sherry) and Triple Cask
  (three-cask vatting). 43% ABV.
- **macallan-triple-cask-15** — the post-2018 name for the
  former Fine Oak 15. Three cask types: European-oak oloroso,
  American-oak oloroso, and American-oak ex-bourbon. The only
  Macallan bottling here with an ex-bourbon component; the
  bourbon-cask path on a distillery whose default identity is
  sherry-led. 43% ABV.

Sourcing caveats
----------------

This entry is unusual in citing only Wikipedia sources. The
producer's own themacallan.com pages, scotchwhisky.com
Whiskypedia, and whisky.com all returned empty content at fetch
time (JS-rendered or CSP-blocked at the WebFetch level).
Wikipedia's article on The Macallan distillery is well-cited
to trade-press and book sources (Master of Malt 2018, MacLean
2008, Kindersley 2008, Bowman 2017) that can be migrated to
directly when those upstream sources become accessible. Per the
project's Wikipedia-volatility policy, this citation is a
starting point; the migration queue is listed in TODO.md
Research Requests.

One Wikipedia-internal inconsistency was resolved in the entry:
the article credits the 2018 still house to "Norman Foster" in
one paragraph and to "Rogers Stirk Harbour + Partners" in the
infobox and the Stirling-Prize-2019 reference. The project
records the architect as RSHP per the better-attributed source,
with the inconsistency noted in the source-1 notes for
verification.
